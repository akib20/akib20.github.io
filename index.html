Profile

I’m a final-year PhD candidate at C2N (CNRS – Université Paris-Saclay), supervised by Damien Querlioz and Louis Hutin. My work takes probabilistic and in-memory computing from single nanodevices to working systems: stochastic-MTJ p-bits, memristor crossbars and their CMOS interfaces.

Day to day that means Cadence circuit design, PCB design and Python-automated characterization at the probe station. I’m currently designing 130 nm ferroelectric memory circuits for a June 2027 tape-out.

I’m co-first author of a Nature Communications paper on a hybrid p-bit/memristor Ising machine and of an IEDM 2026 publicity highlight on fully on-chip ReRAM-to-ReRAM sampling. Across my publications: two Nature Communications papers, three at IEDM, and one at the VLSI Symposium. I also hold Google Cloud Professional Cloud Architect and Generative AI Leader certifications.

Selected work

  • Hybrid memristor–MTJ Ising machine

    Nature Communications, 2026. Co-first author. Stochastic magnetic tunnel junctions integrated with CMOS act as p-bits, and a HfOx memristor crossbar stores multi-level couplings. Annealing comes from the read voltage, so no ADCs are needed.
    • Reached the global optimum in 3 of 3 independent runs, on both a 24-node weighted Max-Cut and a 10-node, 3-color graph-coloring problem (30 spins).
    • Sigmoidal p-bit response held down to 130 ns pulses, supporting update rates above 10 MHz.
    • Memristor couplings drifted by less than 15 µS over one month and still produced the optimal coloring.
    Read the paper

  • Langevin learning with fully on-chip ReRAM sampling

    IEDM 2026, publicity highlight. Co-first author. An experimental demonstration of Metropolis-adjusted Langevin learning in which sampling runs entirely on chip, ReRAM to ReRAM.
    • Selected by IEDM as one of its 2026 publicity highlights.
    • Co-first author with B. W. Walker, with colleagues at C2N, CEA-Leti and partner institutions.
    See it in publications

  • Energy budget of a parallel sMTJ/RRAM Ising machine

    VLSI-TSA 2026. Co-author. A 22 nm projection, built on a Cadence-validated model, of how much energy a parallel sMTJ/RRAM Ising machine needs per solve.
    • Under 36 µW per spin update.
    • 0.6 pJ to 23 nJ per solve across MaxSAT, Max-Cut and graph coloring; under 2.1 nJ for the 24-node Max-Cut.
    • Holds in both Ising and QUBO formulations.
    • Showed that peripheral amplifiers, not the sMTJs or RRAM, dominate power.
    Read the paper

  • On-chip learning in RRAM arrays

    With CEA-Leti. Nature Communications 2026; IEDM 2024 and 2026; VLSI Symposium 2025. Training that happens inside resistive memory arrays instead of on a separate processor.
    • Forward-only learning with month-scale stability (Nature Communications, accepted 2026).
    • Forward–Forward learning using the low-voltage reset of RRAM (IEDM 2024).
    • ReRAM write noise used as gradient perturbation for few-update learning (IEDM 2026).
    • Customized on-chip learning for secure edge AI (VLSI Symposium 2025).
    See all publications

  • Ferroelectric memory tape-out and crossbar test board

    In progress, 2026–2027. New silicon and a new board: ferroelectric non-volatile memory circuits in 130 nm, and a PCB for a memristor-crossbar platform.
    • Ferroelectric NVM circuits in a 130 nm process, designed in Cadence Virtuoso and Spectre, for tape-out in June 2027.
    • A PCB for a memristor-crossbar platform with electrical-to-optical signal conversion and hardware interfacing.

  • Automated device characterization

    Python and PyVISA. The measurement stack behind the papers: instrument control, calibration, characterization and data acquisition.
    • Drives Keysight B1530A, Keysight oscilloscopes and probe stations from Python.
    • Automated voltage, current and magnetic-field sweeps for MTJ/sMTJ and memristor/RRAM devices.
    • Extracts switching probabilities and their distributions, with uncertainty metrics and confidence intervals, to set pass/fail criteria for devices and datasets.
